Prompts

Copy-ready prompts for the ThriveStack MCP Server: complete analyses you can paste into your AI tool and run in one step.

MCP prompts are named workflows your AI tool can list and run. The ThriveStack MCP server does not ship a built-in prompt for Revenue Intelligence today, so this page gives you copy-ready prompts instead. Paste one into any connected AI tool and it runs the whole analysis.
The only built-in ThriveStack MCP prompt is ai_visibility_analyst, for AI search visibility. See the Cited By prompts.
Every tool these prompts use is read-only, so running them never changes your data.

How to use these prompts

  1. Connect your AI tool to ThriveStack. See Setup.
  2. Copy a prompt below and replace anything in [square brackets], such as the month or the account name.
  3. Paste it into a new conversation. The assistant calls the tools in order and answers from your live data.
  4. Follow up in plain language. After a prompt runs, every tool is still available, so you can ask about any number it surfaced.

To reuse a prompt without pasting it each time, save it where your client keeps reusable instructions: a Project’s instructions in Claude, a custom slash command in Claude Code, or a rule in Cursor.

Prompt library

Each prompt names the tools it needs, so you can see where every number comes from.

Revenue

Monthly revenue review

Use when: You need the numbers for a leadership or investor update.

Uses: get_revenue_kpis, get_mrr_movements, get_missed_revenue, get_revenue_goals, get_revenue_goal_progress

Prompt
Act as my revenue analyst. Prepare the monthly revenue review for [month and year].

1. Call get_revenue_kpis and report MRR, ARR, ARPA, net revenue retention, CAC and customer counts, each with its change from the previous month.
2. Call get_mrr_movements for the same period and explain what moved MRR: new, expansion, contraction, churn and reactivation.
3. Call get_missed_revenue and report any revenue missed to failed or missing payments.
4. Call get_revenue_goals, then get_revenue_goal_progress for each active goal, and say whether we are ahead or behind.

Format: a one-paragraph summary first, then a table of KPIs, then three bullets on risks and one on what to do next.
Rules: state the currency once. Use only numbers the tools returned. If a figure is missing, say so rather than estimating.

MRR movement explainer

Use when: MRR went up or down and you want to know who and why.

Uses: get_mrr_movements, list_mrr_movement_customers

Prompt
Explain the change in MRR over [time window, e.g. the last 3 months].

1. Call get_mrr_movements and show new, expansion, contraction, churn and reactivation MRR per month.
2. For the two largest movements in the most recent month, call list_mrr_movement_customers and name the top 5 customers behind each, with plan and amount.
3. Say in two sentences what drove the net change.

Rules: do not guess reasons for churn. Only describe what the data shows, such as plan, amount and date.

Revenue leakage hunt

Use when: You suspect revenue is slipping through failed or missing payments.

Uses: get_missed_revenue, list_missed_payment_customers, list_revenue_account_mappings, get_account_payment_calendar

Prompt
Find revenue we are losing to missed payments.

1. Call get_missed_revenue for the last 6 months and show expected versus missed revenue per month.
2. For the most recent month, call list_missed_payment_customers and list the customers ordered by MRR at risk, with status and days overdue.
3. For the 3 largest, find their billing customer ids with list_revenue_account_mappings, then call get_account_payment_calendar for this year and tell me whether this is a one-off or a pattern.

Format: a short summary with the total MRR at risk, then the table.
Rules: do not contact or change anything. This is a read-only review.

Plan and pricing mix review

Use when: You are reviewing pricing, packaging or which plan to focus on.

Uses: get_plan_mrr_summary, get_plan_mrr_trend, get_acv_summary

Prompt
Review our plan mix.

1. Call get_plan_mrr_summary and show each plan's customers, MRR, ARR and share of customers and MRR.
2. Call get_plan_mrr_trend for the three plans with the most MRR and show how each moved over the last 6 months.
3. Call get_acv_summary and compare average contract value across plans.

Then tell me: which plan carries our revenue, which is growing fastest, and which has many customers but little MRR.
Rules: separate what the data shows from your own interpretation, and label each.

Goal pacing check

Use when: You want to know whether you will hit the revenue targets you set.

Uses: get_revenue_goals, get_revenue_goal_progress, get_revenue_kpis

Prompt
Check whether we are on track for our revenue goals.

1. Call get_revenue_goals and list each active goal with its target and dates.
2. For each, call get_revenue_goal_progress and report expected versus actual for the current month and the variance.
3. Call get_revenue_kpis to confirm the current value of each metric.

Then rank the goals from most behind to most ahead, and say in one sentence what would close the biggest gap.
Rules: if there are no active goals, say so and suggest which metric to set one for.

Marketing

Channel and campaign review

Use when: You are deciding where to put next month's marketing budget.

Uses: get_channel_performance, get_campaign_performance, get_multi_touch_attribution, get_campaign_revenue_ranking

Prompt
Review our marketing channels for the last 30 days.

1. Call get_channel_performance and show visitors, sessions, identified users and accounts per channel.
2. Call get_multi_touch_attribution and compare the revenue each channel gets under first-touch, last-touch and linear attribution.
3. Call get_campaign_performance for the top 2 channels and name the best and worst campaigns.
4. Call get_campaign_revenue_ranking for the best channel and list the campaigns ranked by revenue.

Format: a table of channels, then three recommendations on where to spend more or less.
Rules: use only returned numbers, and flag any channel where the attribution models disagree strongly.

Lead quality check

Use when: You want to know whether recent signups are worth following up.

Uses: get_new_signup_counts, get_lead_quality, list_leads_by_quality, get_acquisition_timing

Prompt
Check the quality of our recent signups.

1. Call get_new_signup_counts and report new users and accounts for the last day, 7 days and 30 days with their change.
2. Call get_lead_quality for the last month and give the mix of corporate, personal-email and abusive or fake leads.
3. Call list_leads_by_quality for corporate leads from the last 7 days and list the 10 newest.
4. Call get_acquisition_timing and tell me how many touches and days a visitor takes to sign up.

End with whether quality is improving, and which source to look at first.
Rules: do not email or contact any lead.

Product analytics

Activation and retention review

Use when: You want to know whether new users reach value and whether they stay.

Uses: get_activation_summary, get_activation_trend, get_feature_conversion_drivers, get_retention_data, get_churn_expansion_signals

Prompt
Review activation and retention for [month and year].

1. Call get_activation_summary and get_activation_trend and report milestone completion and the trend.
2. Call get_feature_conversion_drivers (step modules), pick the activation module with the weakest milestone, then call it again (step milestone) and compare converted and dropped accounts.
3. Call get_retention_data and describe how cohorts return over time.
4. Call get_churn_expansion_signals and report the high, medium and low stickiness split.

Format: findings first, then the one milestone I should work on and why.
Rules: separate what the data shows from your own interpretation.

Funnel scorecard

Use when: You want one view of the funnel from visitors to payers.

Uses: get_monthly_metrics_table, get_mau_mtu_count

Prompt
Build a funnel scorecard for the last 12 months.

1. Call get_monthly_metrics_table (it takes no inputs) and show it as a table, month by month.
2. Call get_mau_mtu_count for the most recent complete month (as YYYY-MM) and add active and tracked users.
3. Point out the month-to-month step with the biggest drop and the one with the biggest gain.

Rules: do not smooth or round away a drop. Say which figures are missing, if any.

Accounts

At-risk account review

Use when: You are preparing the customer success team's weekly list.

Uses: list_at_risk_accounts, get_account_overview, get_account_engagement_trend, get_account_feature_usage, get_account_payment_calendar

Prompt
Prepare our weekly at-risk account review.

1. Call list_at_risk_accounts and take the 5 accounts with the most at stake.
2. For each, call get_account_overview and get_account_engagement_trend, and say whether the risk is engagement, monetization or both.
3. For each, call get_account_feature_usage and say which features they use and how widely.
4. For accounts at risk on monetization, find their billing customer ids and check get_account_payment_calendar for missed payments.

Format: one short paragraph per account, ending with a suggested next step.
Rules: this is read-only. Do not message anyone.

Account brief

Use when: You are preparing for a call, a renewal or a handover for one account.

Uses: list_crm_accounts, get_account_overview, get_account_acquisition, get_account_users, get_account_activity, get_account_revenue

Prompt
Give me a brief on [account name].

1. Find the account with list_crm_accounts. If there is more than one match, ask me which one.
2. Call get_account_overview, then get_account_acquisition to see how they found us.
3. Call get_account_users and get_account_activity for the last 30 days: who is active and what they do.
4. Find the account's billing customer ids with list_revenue_account_mappings and call get_account_revenue for MRR, plan and payment status.

Format: six bullets I can read in under a minute, then anything I should follow up on.
Rules: if the account has no linked billing customer, say so and skip step 4.

Setup

Data health check

Use when: A number looks wrong, or you want to be sure before sharing a report.

Uses: get_setup_status, get_marketing_setup_status, get_revenue_sync_status, list_revenue_customer_mappings, list_revenue_account_mappings

Prompt
Check whether our ThriveStack data can be trusted.

1. Call get_setup_status and get_marketing_setup_status and report what is connected and what is missing.
2. Call get_revenue_sync_status and report the latest sync per source, with status and time. Flag anything failed or stale.
3. Call list_revenue_customer_mappings with filter UNMAPPED and report how many billing customers are not linked to an account, and the biggest few.
4. Call list_revenue_account_mappings with filter UNMAPPED and report how many product accounts have no billing customer.

End with a verdict: safe to report, safe with caveats (list them), or not yet.
Rules: do not fix or change anything.

Write your own

A good prompt has the same few parts:

  • A period: “last 30 days” or “October 2026”. Revenue tools default to the last 6 complete months.
  • The steps, in order: name the tool or the question for each step, then say what to do with the result.
  • An output format: a table, a short summary, or a list ordered by amount.
  • Guardrails: ask it to use only returned numbers, to say when data is missing, and to separate what the data shows from its own interpretation.

For ideas on what to ask, see Use Cases.
